Reading

Study guide 01

True / False / Not Given

Evidence, not assumptions

Core concept

TRUE means the statement agrees with the passage. FALSE means the passage contradicts it. NOT GIVEN means the passage does not establish whether it is true or false.

Strategy

Read the statement carefully; locate the relevant passage; compare the complete meaning; choose only what the text supports.

Worked example

Passage: The library opened in 1998 and expanded its digital collection in 2012.
Quick check: Statement: The library began offering digital books in 1998.
The passage gives the opening year but does not say when digital books were first offered.
Study guide 02

Matching Headings

Main idea versus details

Core concept

A heading should summarize the central purpose of a paragraph, not an isolated fact. Similar wording is not enough.

Strategy

Read each paragraph for its main point; formulate a short summary; compare it against all headings; eliminate options that describe only a detail.

Worked example

Paragraph: Urban gardens offer fresh food, but their greatest contribution may be bringing neighbours together through shared work.
Quick check: Best heading?
The paragraph emphasizes social connection, not just food.
Study guide 03

Completion Questions

Word limits and grammar

Core concept

For text-based completion tasks, follow the exact word limit and use words from the passage where instructed. The completed sentence must make grammatical sense.

Strategy

Check NO MORE THAN instructions; predict the needed word type; scan for the idea; copy accurately; check spelling and number.

Worked example

Passage: The experiment was conducted in a laboratory near the coast. Instruction: NO MORE THAN ONE WORD.
Quick check: The experiment took place in a _____.
Laboratory is the exact one-word answer supported by the passage.
Study guide 04

Skimming & Scanning

Two distinct reading tools

Core concept

Skimming means reading quickly for overall meaning. Scanning means looking for a specific name, number, term or other detail. Neither replaces careful reading of evidence.

Strategy

Skim titles and topic sentences for structure; scan for a target detail; then read its surrounding sentences closely.

Worked example

You need to find the year a researcher published a study. Which skill is most useful first?
Quick check: You need to find the year a researcher published a study. Which skill is most useful first?
Scanning helps locate a specific year.

Writing

Study guide 05

Task 2: Answer the Question

Task response comes first

Core concept

Writing Task 2 requires a response of at least 250 words. The response should address all parts of the prompt and develop relevant ideas with support.

Strategy

Underline instruction words (discuss, agree, advantages); identify each part; choose a position where needed; plan two developed main ideas.

Worked example

Prompt: Discuss both views and give your own opinion. Which plan fits?
Quick check:
The instruction explicitly requires both views and your own opinion.
Study guide 06

Task 2: Develop an Idea

Point → reason → illustration

Core concept

A developed paragraph explains why an idea matters and may illustrate it with a relevant example. Repeating the same claim does not add depth.

Strategy

Make a clear point; explain the mechanism or reason; add a relevant example; link the example back to the argument.

Worked example

Which is better developed?
Quick check:
The second statement gives a concrete explanation.
Study guide 07

Task 1: Describe Data

Overview and comparisons

Core concept

Academic Task 1 asks you to summarize visual information in at least 150 words. Select key features and make relevant comparisons; do not invent explanations unsupported by the visual.

Strategy

Identify the visual and units; write an overview of major patterns; group key details; use accurate figures and comparisons.

Worked example

A line chart shows sales rising from 10 to 30 units. Which description is supported?
Quick check:
Only the numerical change is supported; causes and future trends are not.

Listening

Study guide 08

Predict Before You Listen

Use the question as a guide

Core concept

Before the recording starts, read the task and predict the type of information needed. Listen for meaning rather than only matching individual words.

Strategy

Check word limits; underline the target; predict a name, number or noun; listen for corrections; verify spelling.

Worked example

Form: Arrival date: ____ . What should you listen for?
Quick check:
The blank requires a date.
Study guide 09

Distractors and Corrections

The final answer may change

Core concept

Speakers may correct an earlier detail. Do not write the first number or name you hear without checking what the speaker finally confirms.

Strategy

Notice signals like actually, sorry, I mean, instead; keep listening; record the final confirmed information.

Worked example

Speaker: “The meeting is on Tuesday—sorry, I meant Thursday.”
Quick check:
Thursday is the corrected date.

Speaking

Study guide 10

Part 1: Natural Answers

Answer, extend, personalize

Core concept

In Speaking Part 1, respond directly and extend naturally with a reason or example. Avoid memorized scripts that sound unrelated to the question.

Strategy

Answer the question; add one reason; give a short personal example when useful; speak naturally.

Worked example

Question: Do you enjoy reading? Which answer is stronger?
Quick check:
The second answer develops the response naturally.
Study guide 11

Part 2: Structure a Long Turn

A simple story arc

Core concept

You have one minute to prepare and should aim to speak for up to two minutes. Organize ideas into a clear sequence rather than memorizing a full script.

Strategy

Use preparation time for keywords; introduce the subject; add context and details; explain why it matters; finish naturally.

Worked example

Which notes are most useful for a Part 2 cue card?
Quick check:
Keyword notes support a coherent, spontaneous response.

Vocabulary

Study guide 12

Precision and Collocations

Natural language beats rare words

Core concept

Lexical resource is about range, precision, appropriacy and control. Using unusual words inaccurately can weaken communication.

Strategy

Learn words with their common partners; study example sentences; check register; reuse expressions in your own relevant sentences.

Worked example

Which collocation is natural?
Quick check:
Make a decision is the established collocation.
Study guide 13

Paraphrasing Without Distortion

Keep the meaning

Core concept

Paraphrasing changes wording while preserving the original meaning. Do not replace words with near-synonyms that alter strength, certainty or scope.

Strategy

Identify the core meaning; change sentence structure if helpful; check that qualifiers like some, most and all are preserved.

Worked example

Which paraphrases “Some students prefer online lessons”?
Quick check:
A number of preserves the limited scope of some.
